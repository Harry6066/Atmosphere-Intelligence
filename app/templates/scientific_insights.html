The Jaccard Paradox

While overall binary classification agreement appears deceptively high at 95.33%, this figure is dominated entirely by the shared identification of normal weather days (11,064 shared inliers).

Total Union Anomalies: 624 days
Consensus Overlap: 78 days
Jaccard Overlap Ratio: 78 / 624 = 12.50%

This mathematical divergence confirms that Isolation Forest and LOF probe fundamentally distinct topological properties of the meteorological feature space.

Global vs Local Detection

Isolation Forest operates globally through recursive orthogonal hyper-planes. Points that lie far from the multivariate center (e.g. subzero blizzards with heavy snow) require few splits and receive extreme negative scores.

Local Outlier Factor (LOF) calculates the local reachability density against $k=20$ nearest neighbors. An observation can have moderate absolute values yet be strongly anomalous if located in a sparse region between dense clusters.

Example: The 2021 Pacific Northwest Heat Dome (42.2°C in Seattle) was captured by LOF (3 of 3 days) but missed by IF (0 of 3 days) because Seattle's maritime distribution is locally tight.

Consensus Triage Utility

Because unsupervised models lack ground-truth fault labels, manual domain inspection is expensive. Enforcing a Dual-Model Consensus reduces review workload by 77.8% (from 351 down to 78 observations).

Operational Risks: False Positives & False Negatives in Sensor Quality Control

1. Conflation of Extreme Weather with Faults

Unsupervised models cannot distinguish between a fractured temperature probe reporting -30°C on a summer day and a legitimate historic Arctic Polar Vortex where temperatures legitimately plunge to -30.5°C across the Midwest. Both produce identical high-dimensional sparsity.

2. Risk of Purging Rare Climate Data

If automated quality-control pipelines automatically delete or overwrite flagged observations, rare climate records (the 100-year heat waves, hurricane downpours, and blizzard depths) will be erased from meteorological databases, artificially attenuating climate change variance signals.

3. Calibration Drift Blindness (False Negatives)

Point-in-time anomaly detection with a fixed 3% contamination threshold cannot detect subtle, persistent sensor defects (e.g. a resistance thermometer drifting +0.4°C every year). Such observations remain well within the 97% normal probability envelope.

4. Climate Envelope Relativity

An observation of 32°C (90°F) in Miami is climatologically expected and completely unremarkable, whereas the exact same measurement in Seattle represents an extreme record-breaking heat event that severely strains local infrastructure.

5. Statistical Detector Epistemological Limits

A statistical model only reports mathematical distance from an empirical distribution. Confirming a physical hardware malfunction requires physical calibration benches, dual-instrument co-location, or ground-level technician inspection reports.

6. Ground Truth Absence (No Synthetic Numbers)

Because this study uses real-world observational data from NOAA GHCN-Daily rather than synthetic simulation benchmarks, precise numerical False-Positive and False-Negative Rates cannot be mathematically computed without fabricating arbitrary labels.

Dimensionality Distinction: PCA Visual Lens vs Anomaly Classifiers

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied strictly as an exploratory visual projection lens that compresses 10 standardized features into 2D (44.05% variance) and 3D (56.25% variance) representations. It is never used as an anomaly classifier because it intentionally discards 43.75% of residual variance in components 4 through 10. Isolation Forest and LOF operate exclusively on the complete, lossless 10-dimensional feature space.
